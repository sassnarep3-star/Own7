Leaderboard

Signal and staking in two tables that never touch, so a lucky staking run cannot be mistaken for a good forecast.

0live
0provisional
14paper

Why the two halves are separate

  • The signal table is ranked on evidence strength, never on a return. Its columns are computable with no price at all.
  • The staking table is empty and stays empty. When a price exists it can demote an entry and can never promote one, so a lucky staking run cannot lift a bad signal.
  • No column in either table mixes the two: no return, stake or drawdown appears in the signal table, and no forecast accuracy appears in the staking table.

The failure this prevents is ordinary: a flat stake that lands on a good month produces a large return, the return produces a rank, and the rank gets read as evidence about the forecast. Splitting the tables removes that path. When a price exists the staking table will be able to demote an entry, and will still never be able to promote one.

Ranking metric

signal score: log10(n) x |statistic - reference| x -log10(p). A bare hit rate rewards a small sample, so the score scales the distance from the reference by the evidence for it. A 60% record on 18 selections cannot outrank a 60% record on 120, and a result consistent with chance scores near zero however large the raw gap looks.

Tiers and rewards

  • Paper: Default. Rules and settlement are sound; the signal has not cleared the evidence bar.
  • Provisional: G03, G04, G05 and G08 clear. Published for review; no reward, no stake.
  • Live: Provisional plus G06 (executable price) and G10 (source rights). Only a live strategy is exposed to the demotion gate.
  • Blocked on data: The official numbers needed to grade the strategy were never retrieved, so the performance gates do not apply.
  • Control: Published to demonstrate a property of the test - a forecast graded against its own number, or a slip whose legs are the same event. A control is never ranked and never promoted.
