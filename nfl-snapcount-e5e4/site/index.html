This desk turns the league's own Playtime Percentage table into the one thing the rest of the repository could not supply: an official, per-player snap count.

The official NFL Game Book publishes a Playtime Percentage table - snap counts and snap share on Offense, Defense and Special Teams - for every fixture. This desk transcribes that table from all 13 played Week 10 Game Books, derives the measurable starter/backup split from playing time, and resolves the depth-order source the brief flagged as unresolved. Nothing is inferred; blanks stay blank with a reason.

WHAT THIS CHANGES

What this desk changes

Four requirements move from inference to transcription.

Four open requirements stop being estimates:

  • R11 - depth from playing time, not jersey order. The playing-time depth page ranks every player by the snaps they actually played; jersey number, experience and salary play no part in the order.
  • R31 - official snap counts. The per-game snap records are transcribed from the official Game Book Playtime Percentage table.
  • R52 - the depth-order source is resolved. Snap counts are the official source for who played; the printed depth-chart order is a separate, club-published artefact.
  • R56 - snap counts as the starter proxy. Where a printed depth chart is unavailable, the highest-snap player at a position is the playing-time starter; the replacement-depth page names who is behind him.
BOUNDARIES

What this desk does not claim

The honest limit is stated before the numbers.

The Playtime Percentage table is snap counts, not a depth chart. The desk does not publish a jersey-order depth, does not infer a player's experience or salary, and does not claim the four tables labelled '(Unofficial)' are certified official statistics - the label is carried verbatim. The depth-vs-snap cross-reference against printed club depth charts is a published zero-row gap, because the four clubs with captured depth charts did not play in Week 10.
