S-01 · Decide the data-rights path before building anything bigger

Problem. NFL.com Terms section 1.3 allows individual, non-commercial, informational use only and prohibits systematic retrieval or compilation. Almost every planned feature (automated weekly injury pulls, a priced leaderboard, cash rewards) is on the wrong side of that line today.

Options.

  • A. Stay strictly personal and non-commercial - Keep manual, dated, small-sample reads. Publish only what a person read and attributed. No automation of club or league pages, no redistribution, no rewards.
  • B. Seek written permission or a licence from the league - Ask NFL Enterprises / a licensed data partner in writing for the intended use. Until an answer exists, behaviour is unchanged from A.
  • C. Buy a licensed feed that bundles rights - A commercial statistics and odds vendor usually grants data rights inside the contract. This is the only path that supports a public product at scale, and it costs money and a contract review.

Recommendation. A now, B in parallel, C only if the project is meant to be public or commercial. Do not start automating club pages while the answer is outstanding.

Effort: Low for A, low to start B, high for C · Impact: Unblocks or forecloses every other suggestion · Risk: Building first and asking later guarantees rework and possible takedown

S-02 · Get a timestamped market price source (this is the master unlock)

Problem. Six of the ten gates fail for one reason: there is no price. Without a timestamped price for the exact market and leg, no edge, ROI, closing-line value or ranking can be computed, and the leaderboard stays empty.

Options.

  • A. Licensed odds API with archived closing snapshots - Highest quality: every leg timestamped, retrievable and contractually usable. Enables G4, G5, G6, G7 and a real leaderboard.
  • B. Manual transcription of one operator's posted prices - Small pilot only. The operator's own page is the primary source for its own price, so G1 can pass, but volume is low and the sample gate stays shut for months.
  • C. Keep prices blank - Honest, and it means the competition cannot open this season. Suitable only if the project is a teaching exercise.

Recommendation. B immediately as a pilot with an explicit timestamp on every quote; A if the goal is a real competition this season.

Effort: Medium (B) to high (A) · Impact: Unlocks gates G4, G5, G6, G7 and the leaderboard · Risk: A single operator's price is not the market; pricing off one book is a known source of false edges

S-03 · Build a timestamped pre-game snapshot archive

Problem. The only Wednesday practice report is already published, and the Sunday Game Status Report is not due until Friday 4 p.m. New York time. A price built today is stale by rule, so gate G2 fails.

Options.

  • A. Two snapshots per week per game - Capture the practice report when read and the Game Status Report after its deadline, each with a UTC timestamp and a content hash.
  • B. One snapshot, one hour before kickoff - Cheaper, closest to the real decision point, but loses the trail that shows how information moved.

Recommendation. A, because the delta between the two reports is itself a candidate signal and the archive is what makes G2 passable later.

Effort: Low · Impact: Makes the injury/depth work usable, and makes any future backtest honest · Risk: Snapshots taken by hand are easy to skip; the process needs a checklist

S-04 · Replace 'starter injured' with a measured role weight

Problem. The current analysis says a first-string player is out and names the next man up. That is a description, not a number, and equal-weighting every position would be an invented assumption.

Options.

  • A. Equal weight per first-string absence - Simplest and transparent, but treats kicker and quarterback the same and will not survive contact with a backtest.
  • B. Snap-share weight from official participation counts - Weight each absence by the share of snaps the player normally takes. Objective and auditable if an official participation source is available.
  • C. Market-implied weight - Let the price move caused by the absence define the weight. Circular unless the price is archived before the news, and it needs S-02.

Recommendation. B, with A kept only as a labelled naive baseline. Do not adopt C before an archive exists.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Turns the injury page into a model input instead of a news feed · Risk: Snap counts are a proxy for importance, not for replacement quality

S-05 · Extend depth coverage from 3 clubs to the whole slate - carefully

Problem. Depth charts were read for Arizona, Cleveland and San Francisco only, so 25 of the 28 clubs playing this week have no depth evidence. The three-way join (injury status, depth order, prior role) currently works on a third of a slate.

Options.

  • A. Manual read of the remaining 25 club charts this week - Highest fidelity, roughly one to two hours, and stays inside a personal read of a public page.
  • B. Script the club pages - Fast, but conflicts with the no-systematic-retrieval term and would need S-01 settled first.
  • C. Leave the gap and label it - Defensible and already published as a flagged gap, but the depth signal stays a pilot.

Recommendation. A this week, B only after written permission, C only if the week is already gone.

Effort: Low (A) to medium (B) · Impact: Makes the depth-deficiency strategy usable across a full slate · Risk: Club charts are updated at different times and are not league-normalised

S-06 · Lock the competition rules before anyone competes

Problem. A leaderboard that ranks strategies without a declared family, a minimum sample and a skill baseline rewards luck and will be gamed by whoever tests the most variants.

Options.

  • A. Brier skill score versus the control model, Holm-adjusted, minimum 100 settled bets - Published now, before results exist. Losing to the control means the strategy is rejected, not ranked.
  • B. Return-based ranking (ROI) - Intuitive but dominated by variance at small samples and trivially improvable by taking long-odds parlays.
  • C. Both, with Brier as the qualifying score and ROI as a displayed diagnostic - Combines a stable qualifier with an intuitive number that is explicitly not the ranking.

Recommendation. C. Never rank on ROI alone at these sample sizes.

Effort: Low · Impact: Prevents the leaderboard from being meaningless · Risk: Any change to the rules after results arrive invalidates the comparison

S-07 · Build a settled-leg dataset before claiming any parlay correlation

Problem. The five published correlations are team-season statistics. They do not tell you how a spread leg and a total leg co-move in the same game, which is the only correlation that matters for a same-game parlay.

Options.

  • A. Collect settled margin and total pairs for past games - One row per game with final score and both leg results. Even 200 games gives usable joint rates and a correlation interval.
  • B. Simulate legs from the rating model - Fast and wrong: it measures the model's assumption, not the world's correlation.
  • C. Assume zero correlation - The standard parlay error. It systematically misprices correlated legs in both directions.

Recommendation. A. Leave the leg cells blank until it exists, exactly as they are blank today.

Effort: Medium · Impact: Unlocks gate G9 and makes parlay pricing defensible · Risk: Historical results alone do not give you the price that was available at the time

S-08 · Treat the player-prop pipeline as a separate project

Problem. Prop combos need official player game logs, a prop price and a settlement rule per market. None of the three exists in this repository.

Options.

  • A. Start with one market and one position - Quarterback passing yards, using official logs and a single operator's posted line. Small scope, checkable end to end.
  • B. Wait for the licensed feed - Correct if props are the goal and a contract is coming. Keeps the repo clean meanwhile.
  • C. Publish a settlement-rule register first - No numbers, just the rules that decide whether a leg wins: injury voids, overtime, minimum participation.

Recommendation. C, then A. Settlement rules decide the outcome more often than the model does, and a void rule can flip a 'win' into a push.

Effort: Low (C) to high (A) · Impact: Prevents a prop strategy from being scored against the wrong settlement rule · Risk: Operator rules differ, so a single register must record the operator, not just the market

S-09 · Add a weekly injury-delta engine

Problem. Wednesday practice participation is not the decision-time state. Between Wednesday and kickoff, designations appear, players are ruled out and backups are named.

Options.

  • A. Diff the three weekly reports and publish only the changes - Small table, high signal, and it shows whether a player is trending better or worse rather than just 'limited'.
  • B. Keep one snapshot per week - Cheap, but it hides the most useful part: the direction of travel.

Recommendation. A, once S-03 exists. The Friday Game Status Report deadline is published, so the schedule for this is known in advance.

Effort: Low · Impact: Turns injury tracking into a weekly process rather than a page · Risk: Missed deadlines silently create gaps; the engine must record a miss as a miss

S-10 · Adopt a decision log so every choice is reversible

Problem. Suggestions are being made faster than they are decided. Without a log, a rejected idea comes back next week and an accepted one has no rollback point.

Options.

  • A. One page, one row per decision - ID, date, decision, owner, rationale, revisit date, rollback. Fits the existing site pattern and costs almost nothing.
  • B. Git commit messages only - Free, but hard for a non-technical reader to review.

Recommendation. A. It is the mechanism that lets you 'choose the best path' rather than accumulate suggestions.

Effort: Very low · Impact: Makes the suggestion register usable · Risk: A log nobody updates is worse than none; tie each row to a commit

S-11 · Separate NFL content from the historical NBA and rental sections

Problem. The repository now holds three research programmes (NBA, San Francisco rental, NFL) behind one navigation and one generated-build pipeline. Readers cannot tell which era a claim belongs to, and the front page still describes an NBA lab.

Options.

  • A. One site, clearly labelled sections with a status banner per section - Keep the shared checks and the single build; add an explicit 'archived / active' label to each section.
  • B. Separate repositories - Cleanest separation, but duplicates the test harness and loses the shared verification tooling.
  • C. Delete the older sections - Not acceptable: it discards reviewed work and its audit trail.

Recommendation. A. Label first, split later only if the sections start to conflict.

Effort: Low · Impact: Removes the largest source of reader confusion · Risk: Relabelling can look like a claim change; keep the original pages and add status, do not rewrite history

S-12 · Publish an explicit responsible-gambling block beside every price

Problem. The site is moving toward prices and a competition, but the support resources and the legal position are not attached to the places where numbers appear.

Options.

  • A. One standard block on every page that shows a line - Age and jurisdiction limits, the fact that no strategy here is qualified, and a link to support services.
  • B. One page, linked once - Lighter, but easy to miss at the point of use.

Recommendation. A. Prices without that block are the single most likely source of harm in this project.

Effort: Very low · Impact: Harm reduction and credibility · Risk: A support line must be official and current; do not invent one
