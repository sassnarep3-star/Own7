## Answers to the five questions
1. **Reasonable/normal increase:** up to **1.6%** per year per unit under the local ordinance (the controlling law); the state 8.8% figure and any federal notion of a cap do not govern these units.
2. **Corporations vs individual owners:** identical rules under Admin Code Ch. 37 for these pre-1979 units; corporate ownership only affects AB 1482 exemptions for single-family homes/condos (not applicable here).
3. **Correct notice:** rent increase license via Housing Inventory **before** the increase; written notice 30 days (90 if >10%, +5 if mailed); use the percentage in effect on the effective date; lawful reason = annual allowable increase (+ any properly banked amounts); above-cap only via Rent Board petition.
4. **Tenant-lawyer review:** a compliant increase (within cap, once/12 months, proper notice, valid license) is normal business practice and passes review; non-conforming increases are null and void, with misdemeanor exposure. Free tenant help: SF Rent Board counseling (415) 252-4600; Eviction Defense Collaborative / Right to Counsel (415) 659-9184. [sf.gov resources](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-and-landlord-resources)
5. **One unit or both:** no law requires raising both; the defensible practice is the **same lawful percentage and procedure for all tenants**, avoiding disparate-treatment claims under SF/California/federal fair-housing law (protected classes incl. source of income). [sf.gov discrimination](https://www.sf.gov/information--get-help-discrimination-housing)
