# OWN7 NFL acceptance review — 2026-11-18

**Disposition: research/shadow use only.** This is an independent repository acceptance review, not a legal opinion, a price feed, a wagering recommendation, or evidence that any strategy is profitable.

## Executive summary

The repository already contains a substantial NFL research workspace: a GitHub Pages landing dashboard, separate pages for originated lines, results, injury/availability evidence, validation gates, strategy status, questions, suggestions, and flagged items. It is reproducible and the static-site and browser checks pass on the reviewed tree. The official results corpus in the repository covers 150 2026 regular-season games through Week 10, with 134 games scored by the walk-forward analysis.

The results are **not** enough to operate a betting competition with rewards. The current results engine reports 82–52 (61.2%) for the directional spread board and 66–68 (49.3%) for totals. It reports a single-test spread p-value of 0.0060, BH q-value of 0.0864, and a separate family-wise false-positive estimate of 0.1023 for its 18-strategy family. These are derived, single-season backtest statistics—not official NFL statistics, market-price comparisons, or prospective evidence. The engine reports **0 qualified, 1 provisional, and 29 rejected** variants; the leaderboard page exposes only the provisional status and no backtest numbers. No strategy is cleared for live use or reward.

The main blockers are still substantive:

1. **Rights and price policy are unresolved.** The price store and fair/market/difference columns exist, but operator market prices are blank. The official NFL terms page is the policy source; the repository records no separate written permission for systematic capture, redistribution, or commercial use. The one checked `nfl.com/odds/` URL is a negative result for that URL only; it does not prove that no price exists anywhere else on the domain.
2. **Prospective and multi-season evidence is absent.** One season can support a bounded retrospective check, not a final train/validation/holdout split or a claim of durable skill.
3. **Availability is not a complete point-in-time graph.** Week 10 Game Books provide actual-starter/inactive evidence, but expected starters, transaction-aware rosters, reserve/IR status, and a permitted multi-day injury-report series are not joined for all clubs and weeks. Do not treat a missing report row as evidence of availability.
4. **Depth and player-combo evidence remain limited.** Club depth material is not a complete, verified 32-team playing-time series. Spread/total dependence does not establish player-prop joint probabilities.
5. **The public decision surfaces are not fully synchronized.** A dated 2026-11-18 summary now exists at `docs/nfl-executive-summary-2026-11-18.md` and README links to it. However, the public navigation still points to `executive-summary.html`, whose copy shows the earlier 77–43 / 64.2% / n=114 analysis, and the machine-readable readiness ledger still contains stale gates. This is a content synchronization/discoverability issue, not a failure of the HTML/link checker.

**Recommended path:** keep the site in shadow/research mode, keep every reward and wager field blank, and do not automate source capture until an authorized source and written data policy are established. Then freeze one singles-only baseline prospectively; collect only permitted point-in-time availability and price evidence; and let proper-score, uncertainty, holdout, rights, and settlement gates determine whether a leaderboard entry can advance. Parlays and prop combos stay blocked until their joint probabilities and void/push rules are directly validated.

## Suggestions for the next decision cycle

These are recommendations for review, not silent approvals or changes already implemented.

1. **Default to “no live wagering, no rewards” until cleared.** The present evidence supports a research dashboard only. The repo does not hold a rights decision or an operator price set. If authorization is not obtained, formally keep the pricing/reward branch disabled rather than treating the missing data as a temporary technical bug.
2. **Reconcile the executive surfaces from one canonical machine-readable ledger.** Generate the landing-page requirement table, executive summary KPIs, and current-status badges from a single versioned source. Add tests for row counts, latest completed week, number scored, correlation n, and readiness text so a results update cannot leave older numbers at the top of the site.
3. **Keep the first competition division singles-only and shadow-only.** Pre-register the rule, benchmarks, target sample, scoring method, uncertainty interval, multiplicity correction, and demotion gate before the prospective window begins. Publish fair probabilities and model inputs, not just picks. Do not pay an entry based on hit rate or backtest ROI.
4. **Build availability as evidence, not a penalty.** Once source rights allow it, persist dated injury reports, club charts, transactions/reserve evidence, inactives, official actual starters, and playing time with source URL, capture time, hashes, and conflict flags. Treat missing records as unknown; model injury *surprises* and compare identical models with/without verified availability features on chronological holdouts.
5. **Gate parlays and prop combos on joint evidence.** Use direct joint observations at the exact legs/thresholds, adequate sample size, calibrated joint probability, and known operator settlement/void rules. A product of marginal probabilities is not a validated combo model.
6. **Make the public dashboard smaller and decision-first.** Put a short current summary, readiness blockers, and links to “Evidence,” “Model & lines,” “Availability,” “Validation,” “Suggestions,” and “Flagged for review” first. Keep the many dated/specialist desk pages available under an archive/category index.
