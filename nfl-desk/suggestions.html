Proposals in full

SG-01

Publish a decision register before publishing any strategy

Problem
The site had strategy tables and validation gates but no single place listing which questions must be answered, by whom, and what evidence would answer them. Readers had to reconstruct the blockers from prose.
Proposal
Keep a register of every open question with its current answer, the evidence held, the evidence missing and an owner. Publish it as its own page and link it from every strategy row.
Rationale
A decision that cannot be traced to its open questions gets made on vibes. The register also makes it visible when the honest answer is 'we do not know'.
Evidence
This repository's own review: the 22 open review flags were spread across eight pages with no single entry point.
Effort
Medium — a data file plus one generated page; no new dependency.
Risk
The register decays into a museum if it is not regenerated by the build. Mitigation: it is generated, tested, and a stale file fails CI.
Gate it serves
Must exist before any strategy is ranked (supports G01, G08). (gates)

SG-02

Keep a reviewable suggestions board instead of implementing improvements directly

Problem
Improvement ideas were being applied as they occurred, so there was no record of what was considered and rejected, and no way to compare two options before choosing.
Proposal
One page, one row per suggestion, with problem, proposal, rationale, effort, risk, the gate it serves, a source link and a disposition the owner sets. Nothing on the page is auto-applied.
Rationale
It gives the owner a choice between paths before any change lands, which is exactly what was asked for, and it leaves an audit trail of declined ideas.
Evidence
Requested directly by the owner in the 2026-11-12 brief.
Effort
Low — data file plus one generated page.
Risk
Suggestions can pile up faster than they are decided. Mitigation: every row shows a disposition, so an undecided backlog is visible rather than hidden.
Gate it serves
Supports G08 (declared trial count) by recording what was tried. (gates)

SG-03

Archive the injury report with timestamps instead of one page state

Problem
The official injury page shows only the current state of the week's report. Without an archive, no one can tell what was knowable at a given kickoff, so no availability strategy can ever pass the temporal gate.
Proposal
Capture each day's report as a dated snapshot with the capture time recorded, and store it separately from the rendered page. One file per capture, hashed.
Rationale
This is the single highest-value data addition available from an official source, and it is the blocker named in gate G02 for every availability strategy.
Evidence
The Week 10 page read on 2026-11-12 has no per-report timestamp in its content, and four clubs on bye are simply absent, which makes a 'no news' club indistinguishable from a club not yet reported.
Effort
Medium — a capture script plus a dated file convention.
Risk
NFL.com Terms §1.3 restricts systematic retrieval and compilation. Any automated capture must be reviewed against those terms before it runs, which is why this is a proposal and not a build.
Gate it serves
Unblocks G02 and G03 for division D-D. (gates)
Impact
High

SG-04

Record which clubs publish a usable depth chart, and never infer one

Problem
NFL.com club pages under /teams/<club>/depth-chart return a 404 for at least one club tested, while the club's own domain serves a chart. Coverage is therefore uneven and cannot be assumed.
Proposal
Keep a per-club coverage table: chart present or absent, the exact URL that worked, and the number of strings listed. Absent clubs stay blank rather than being filled from another source.
Rationale
The availability analysis is only as good as its coverage, and a reader must be able to see which clubs are simply not covered.
Evidence
https://www.nfl.com/teams/washington-commanders/depth-chart returned the site's 404 page on 2026-11-12; https://www.commanders.com/team/depth-chart served a full chart. Three club charts are transcribed here, 29 are not.
Effort
Low for the table, medium to expand coverage club by club.
Risk
Club depth charts are unofficial coaching estimates and change without notice, so the archive date matters as much as the content.
Gate it serves
Raises coverage for G03; does not by itself satisfy it. (gates)
Impact
Medium

SG-05

Separate 'listed on the report' from 'will not play' in every availability display

Problem
A player appearing on the injury report is not the same as a player missing the game. Mixing the two inflates the apparent injury pressure on a club.
Proposal
Show four distinct columns: listed, did not practise, carries a game status, and Out. Never collapse them into one 'injuries' number.
Rationale
It is the difference between a signal and noise. A club with eleven listed players and zero Out designations is not depleted.
Evidence
Week 10: 26 clubs listed a total of 276 player rows, but only a small minority carried any game status at all. Two clubs rendered 'No Injuries Reported' and four were on bye.
Effort
Low — column design in the generator.
Risk
More columns are harder to scan. Mitigation: default sort by game status, with the raw counts available.
Gate it serves
Improves G02 interpretation; prevents a false availability signal. (gates)
Impact
High

SG-06

Treat an unreproducible published average as missing, not as data

Problem
Four of 32 rows in the official team passing table show a per-attempt average that cannot be reproduced from that same table's attempts and yards.
Proposal
Compute every derived rate from the integers and publish the computed value labelled as derived. Where the page's own figure disagrees, exclude the page figure and record the discrepancy as a review item.
Rationale
A rate whose definition cannot be confirmed is not verifiable, and publishing it invites a reader to treat a rounding artefact as a statistic.
Evidence
KC 2037 yards / 263 attempts = 7.745, page shows 7.8; JAX 8.546 shown as 8.6; SEA 7.253 shown as 7.2; PIT 6.452 shown as 6.4. The rounding direction is inconsistent, so the definition of the column is unknown.
Effort
Low.
Risk
Derived rates will differ in the last decimal from the published ones, which needs a visible note to avoid looking like an error.
Gate it serves
Supports G01 (provenance) and G10 (reproducibility). (gates)
Impact
Medium
Disposition

SG-07

Never publish a division rank that needs a tie-breaker

Problem
Division standing is not the same as win-loss order. Head-to-head, division record, common games and strength of victory are applied before points differential.
Proposal
Show a plain record rank from win percentage, explicitly labelled as a record rank, and leave division rank blank where a tie-breaker would be required.
Rationale
Publishing an inferred division order would be exactly the fabrication this project is built to avoid.
Evidence
The official tiebreaking procedure lists twelve ordered steps, none of which are in the standings snapshot held here.
Effort
Low.
Risk
Readers may expect a division column. Mitigation: a one-line explanation beside the table.
Gate it serves
Supports G01. (gates)
Impact
Medium

SG-08

Price nothing, and say so in the masthead

Problem
A research site that shows model output next to a sport invites the reader to assume a price is implied.
Proposal
Keep every price, hold, ROI and closing-line field blank and state the reason once, prominently, with the source-rights citation.
Rationale
The alternative is to display fair-odds numbers that look executable but are not, which is how research sites mislead people.
Evidence
NFL.com publishes no odds. The repository holds no licensed price feed. NFL terms do not grant redistribution or gambling-related rights.
Effort
Already implemented; needs to stay implemented.
Risk
The site looks emptier than competitors. That is the correct trade for a verification-first project.
Gate it serves
Required by G06 and G01. (gates)

SG-09

Make the anti-luck mechanism structural rather than editorial

Problem
Written promises about not rewarding luck do not survive contact with a leaderboard that someone wants to win.
Proposal
Build the scoring so a lucky entry cannot reach the top: rank on a lower confidence bound, require a frozen forward period, and require a declared trial count.
Rationale
A rule enforced by the build cannot be waived in a good week.
Evidence
Three scoring choices are already specified in the competition ledger; none is implemented because no entry is eligible.
Effort
Medium.
Risk
With small samples the lower bound is very punishing and the board may stay empty for a long time. That is the intended behaviour.
Gate it serves
Implements G07, G08 and G09 for the board. (gates)
Impact
High

SG-11

Show the data you do not have, next to the data you do

Problem
Every dashboard lists what it collected. Almost none lists what it decided not to collect, which is where the real limitations live.
Proposal
Add a coverage table per category: source available, source read, rows captured, rows excluded and why.
Rationale
It converts an invisible gap into a reviewable one and stops readers assuming full coverage.
Evidence
This repository captures 3 of 32 club depth charts, 1 week of injury reports, and 0 price observations.
Effort
Low.
Risk
Unflattering. That is the point.

SG-12

Add a second independent source for every official figure before calling it verified

Problem
A single page read once is a transcription, not a verification. Rounding artefacts in the passing table show that a single source can contain internal inconsistencies.
Proposal
For each figure that drives a conclusion, require a second, independent official source, or mark it 'single-source transcription'.
Rationale
It is the difference between 'we read the page' and 'we checked the number'.
Evidence
The passing-average discrepancy would have gone unnoticed without an internal recomputation.
Effort
Medium to high.
Risk
Second sources for official NFL figures are scarce and often reproduce the same upstream feed, which would give false comfort. The badge must say which kind of check was done.
Gate it serves
Strengthens G01. (gates)
Impact
High

Guidance

How to choose between them

  • If the goal is to learn fastest: SG-05 and SG-06. Both are cheap, both fix a place where the current site could mislead a reader.
  • If the goal is to make origination possible at all: SG-03 and SG-04 are the prerequisites, and both are limited by the rights question rather than by effort.
  • If the goal is to trust the leaderboard: SG-09. It turns the anti-luck promise into code that cannot be waived in a good week.
  • If the goal is credibility: SG-10 and SG-12. A verification project whose own descriptions are stale has already lost the argument.
