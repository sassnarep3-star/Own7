Q03

Who replaces a first-string player, and is that replacement a second, third or fourth string option?

This is the owner's stated requirement: know when a backup is covering for a starter.

Answer today
Partly answered. For three clubs (WAS, NYG, SEA) the club's own published depth chart is transcribed, so the listed order at each position is known and the next name is the chart's own replacement. For the other 29 clubs no chart is held, so nothing is claimed.
Information held
154 chart rows across three clubs, joined to the Week 10 injury report by club and player name.
Information missing
Depth charts for 29 clubs; snap share; confirmation that a chart position equals a starting role. Club charts are unofficial and revised silently.

Discovery

Missing-information discovery: what to look for next

  • Ask the rights holder in writing whether a bounded, attributed, non-commercial research snapshot is acceptable, and whether the answer differs for a betting-adjacent audience. Until an answer exists, everything else is provisional.
  • Ask what a licensed price archive costs. The blocker is commercial, not technical: a feed with timestamps and settlement rules converts four Unknown rows into answerable ones.
  • Ask whether depth charts may be captured per club and archived with dates. Coverage is a finite list of thirty-two URLs, most of which resolve on club domains.
  • Ask what the minimum forward window is that would satisfy you as evidence of skill. Deciding this now prevents moving the goal after a bad week.
  • Ask who reviews a candidate that reaches the board, and whether an independent rebuild is available. Self-review is not verification.
