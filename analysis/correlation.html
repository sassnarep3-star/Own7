04 / CORRELATION

Parlay legs are not independent. Here is how much, and how sure we are.

Measured on 18 real games from NBA.com. The result is a genuine measurement — and its confidence interval is the reason it is not yet priced into a bet.

!
The honest headline is the interval, not the point estimate. Home and away scoring correlate at r = 0.42, but the 95% interval runs -0.06 to 0.74 — it includes zero. At 18 games, "these legs are dependent" and "these legs are independent" cannot be told apart. Pinning a correlation to ±0.15 needs roughly 174 paired games: a full season.

WHAT EACH ROW MEANS

Reading the table properly

Home points vs away points — a real measurement

r = 0.420, 95% CI [-0.06, 0.74]. Both teams' scores rise together because they share a game environment: possessions, pace, officiating, and overtime. A same-game parlay on two overs is therefore not independent, and multiplying the two prices would overstate the fair parlay price.

Team points vs game total — deliberately flagged as inflated

r = 0.873 looks spectacular and means nothing: the total is partly made of the team's own points. This row is included precisely to show the difference between a real relationship and a tautology.

Pre-game pace vs realised total — a legitimate forecast check

r = 0.558, 95% CI [0.12, 0.81]. This uses only team PACE as published before the games, so it is an out-of-sample-ish signal rather than a same-game identity. It is the most encouraging number on this page, and it is still far too wide to trade.

Binary-to-binary dependence (prop-combo form)

φ = 0.447 between "home team scores 110+" and "home team wins", 95% CI [-0.02, 0.76]. This is the shape of a player-prop combo problem — two yes/no legs that share a game — and it too cannot be separated from independence yet.

Player vs player prop correlation — blank, on purpose

NBA.com player box-score pages are rendered client-side and returned no table when fetched, so there are no paired player stat lines to correlate. Rather than estimate a coefficient from nothing, the cell is blank and the blocker is documented. See Flagged for review.

SAMPLE SIZE

Why one month of data cannot price a parlay

95% half-width on r ≈ 1.96 / (√(n − 3) × (1 + r²/2)) → n ≈ 3 + (1.96 / half-width)²

To get a correlation interval of ±0.15 around r = 0.3, you need about 174 independent paired games. To get ±0.10, about 390. And games are not fully independent draws, so the true requirement is worse than that. This is the quantitative reason a correlation estimate is not an edge.
