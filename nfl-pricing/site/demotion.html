Demotion gate

A strategy that ranks has to keep earning its place. This is the written rule that sends one back to paper, and the record of what it did to every variant in front of it.

The gate

DG01 Return a live strategy to paper when any of: (a) its signal hit rate falls to or below 50% over a trailing 40-selection window; (b) its exchangeability p-value on the trailing window exceeds 0.50, i.e. the forecast has stopped disaggregating from noise; (c) its staking drawdown exceeds 10 flat units while the signal score is below the 50% break-even line; (d) it loses price agreement - the mean absolute difference between its fair line and the market exceeds 6 points for two consecutive windows, which means it has stopped being a forecast and started being an opinion.

Each clause targets a different failure. (a) and (b) catch a decaying model. (c) catches the exact case the split exists for, a losing signal kept alive by a hot staking run. (d) catches divergence from the market, which is a data or specification break rather than bad luck.

  • Window: 40 settled selections
  • Maximum drawdown before review: 10 flat units
  • Maximum tolerated price disagreement: 6 points
  • Declared before grading: True

No variant was ever live, so every outcome here is a paper outcome. The gate has never fired against a promotion, which is itself an open item (PQ11): a rule that has only ever seen losing strategies is not yet shown to work.

Why a gate and not a feeling

A strategy that has been promoted is exactly the one nobody wants to demote, so the decision is written down before the pressure arrives. The clauses are checkable against the record, and a breach is reported with the number that triggered it.

The gate is deliberately blunt. It cannot tell a genuine cold streak from a broken model, and it does not try: it stops the question being decided by whoever is most attached to the result.
