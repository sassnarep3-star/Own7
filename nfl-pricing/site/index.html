What the numbers say, in one screen

  1. The market knows more than we do. The published close correlates -0.439 with the actual margin (n=136). Our fair line correlates -0.2227 (n=120): the same sign, about half the information.
  2. Our total points the wrong way. The market's total correlates 0.3771 with the actual total; ours correlates -0.0418. That is a defect in our model, and it is published as one rather than tuned away on the same games.
  3. Nothing ranks. 12 variants were graded, 11 clear the selection floor, and none clears multiplicity control. To see a 55% edge at 80% power takes 617 settled selections; the store holds 136 games.
  4. The split did not flatter us here. The best staking record is also the best signal record (S07, 5.909 units, hit rate 0.5763), so separating the two did not promote a different entry. What it did expose is the opposite case: variants that clear break-even on hit rate while their units column is negative.
  5. Depth is counted, not scored. 65 injured players are already the last name printed at their position, and 129 more have a replacement who is himself last on the chart. Both are counts of printed slots, not points.

What this desk does not do

It places no wager, opens no account, holds no bankroll and produces no monetary reward. It does not claim an edge: the honest reading of the leaderboard is that the sample is too small to tell. Where a value is unknown, the cell is blank and the item is on the flagged page, so a reader can see the hole instead of a plausible number.

Read the suggestions before relying on anything here: each one states what would make it fail.

Paper research only. No wager, stake, bankroll or monetary reward is produced anywhere on this desk. A market line here is a published reference price and not an executable offer, and no edge is claimed.
