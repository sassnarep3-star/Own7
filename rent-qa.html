What is a reasonable and normal rent increase in SF 94122 under local, state and federal law?Q1Local (controls here): the SF Rent Board allowable increase is 1.6% for increases effective March 1, 2026 - February 28, 2027, once per 12 months, on base rent. State: AB 1482's cap (8.8% for the SF region, Aug 1, 2026 - Jul 31, 2027) does NOT apply because these units are already under the lower local cap. Federal: there is no federal rent cap; federal law (Fair Housing Act) governs discrimination only. So a normal, defensible increase is up to 1.6% per year.SF-RATES ↗SF-RENT-INCREASES ↗SF-AB1482 ↗CA-CIVIL-1947 ↗HUD-FHEO ↗
How do big real estate corporations handle increases vs individual owners in SF?Q2The same SF Rent Ordinance (Admin Code Ch. 37) applies to both: same annual allowable percentage, same 12-month rule, same notice and license requirements. Corporate ownership changes nothing for these pre-1979 units; it only matters for AB 1482 exemptions on single-family homes/condos, which do not apply here.SF-RENT-INCREASES ↗CA-CIVIL-1947 ↗CA-COSTA-HAWKINS ↗
What is the correct way to inform tenants, and what are reasonable reasons?Q3Obtain the rent increase license (report units to the Rent Board Housing Inventory) BEFORE imposing an increase. Give written notice: 30 days for increases of 10% or less, 90 days if more than 10% (alone or combined in the same year); add 5 days if mailed. Use the percentage in effect on the effective date. The lawful reason is the annual allowable increase (and any properly banked amounts); anything above the cap requires a Rent Board petition (e.g., approved capital improvements).SF-RENT-INCREASES ↗CA-CIVIL-827 ↗
Can the increase pass review by SF tenant advocates and free tenant lawyers?Q4Yes if it complies with Ch. 37: within the published allowable percentage, once per 12 months, proper written notice, and a valid rent increase license. An increase that does not conform to the ordinance is null and void; violations can be misdemeanors with a mandatory $1,000 fine. Tenants can get free help: SF Rent Board counseling (415) 252-4600 and the Eviction Defense Collaborative / Right to Counsel (415) 659-9184.SF-RENT-INCREASES ↗SF-TENANT-RESOURCES ↗
Raise one unit's rent or both, to avoid discrimination claims?Q5No law requires raising both units. But applying the SAME lawful percentage and procedure to all tenants in the building is the defensible, non-discriminatory practice. SF, state (FEHA) and federal (FHA) fair-housing laws prohibit treating tenants differently based on protected classes (race, national origin, familial status, disability, sex, source of income, etc.). Uniform treatment avoids disparate-treatment exposure.SF-DISCRIMINATION ↗HUD-FHEO ↗SF-RENT-INCREASES ↗
