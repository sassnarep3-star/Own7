Official-sourced numbers only, every value carrying its n, with Holm adjustment and every dropped pair published.

The rule this register follows

Correlations are computed only from officially sourced numbers, and every value publishes its n. Two families are kept apart because they answer different questions:

  • Market correlation — what the books price together. The input a parlay needs.
  • Outcome correlation — what actually happens together, from official final scores. A property of football.

The gap between the two is the mispricing a correlated combo could exploit. It cannot be measured at 1 of 4 pairs being adequately powered, and the register shows that rather than hiding it.

Holm-adjusted across the pairs tested in this register. A pair with n below 30 is shown with its n and is not ranked: at n=15 the 95% interval on r spans most of the range, so the point estimate carries almost no information.

What the numbers say

  • Model versus market on the total: r = 0.62 at n=15. Positive and, on p alone, significant — but unpowered. It says the model tracks the market on scoring level, which is a calibration observation, not an edge.
  • Model versus market on the margin: r = 0.91 at n=15. The model follows the market closely while still being less accurate than it. Tracking a good forecast imperfectly is a good way to be worse than it.
  • Final margin versus final total: r = -0.16 at n=136. The only adequately powered result here, and it does not survive Holm adjustment. On this sample, a blowout does not reliably arrive with a high total.
  • Market spread versus market total: r = 0.09 at n=15. Essentially nothing, at a sample too small to say so confidently. This is exactly the pair a correlated parlay would be sold against, and the desk cannot yet price that relationship.
