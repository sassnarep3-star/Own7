Signal and staking, on separate pages of the ledger

A strategy that makes money on a lucky stake pattern must not be able to look skilful. Here that is a property of the code: promotion reads the signal tier only, and the promotion function raises if a staking field reaches it.

Both entered strategies are on paper. Neither is promotable. The side strategy is the more instructive case: its p-value clears 0.05 while its staking ROI is -74.6%. Either figure alone would mislead, and in opposite directions, which is the reason they are reported on separate pages of the ledger.

Why the side strategy is on paper, and what would move it

The side strategy went 2-13 at the open, for a p of 0.007385 against a coin. That is statistically significant and completely useless as evidence of skill, for three reasons the desk prints beside it:

  1. It is significant in the wrong direction. The model expected 9.26 wins from its own probabilities and got 2. Being significantly worse than your own forecast is still a finding, but it is a finding about a broken model, not about an edge.
  2. The model is less accurate than the price. Margin error 13.552 against 10.007. A forecast can only beat a price it is better than, and this one is not.
  3. n is 15, not 200. A significant result on fifteen bets says the direction was consistent on this sample. It does not say the direction would repeat.

What would move the strategy toward live is not a better record; it is positive closing-line value, a powered sample, and a model whose error is below the market's. None of those is available on this capture.

What the promotion rules are

A strategy moves from paper to live only when all five hold. The list is in the committed analysis file and is read by the code, not transcribed by hand.

Staking fields are explicitly excluded and the code raises if one is passed. That exclusion is what makes 'a lucky staking run cannot mask a bad signal' true rather than asserted.
