The finding, stated plainly

The side model went 2-13 at the open and lost 74.6% at flat stake. It is not unlucky. It is wrong. Three independent measurements agree:

  1. It is worse than the market. Margin error 13.552 points RMSE against the market’s 10.007; total error 14.539 against 14.061. A forecast with more error than the price should lose to the price.
  2. It underperformed its own forecast. Its probabilities expected 9.26 wins and produced 2: an excess of -7.26 against expectation, at p = 0.000167. The model was not merely unlucky in its predictions; it was overconfident.
  3. The sample is far too small to rescue it either way. Detecting a 55% rate needs 783 settled bets; this capture has 15.

The total board is a quieter version of the same story: 6-8 for -18.2%, with 6 over picks and 8 under picks. It adds noise rather than making a directional claim. Neither board is an edge; the difference is that one is visibly wrong and the other merely uninformative.

A note on how this finding was reached. The first build of this desk had a sign error: it compared the model’s expected home margin against the home club’s printed spread rather than the market’s implied margin, which inverted every side pick. That version went 13-2 and looked excellent. The error was caught by hand-checking a single game against its official score, fixed, and two regression tests now pin the convention. The discovery is reported because a desk that quietly corrects itself in private is not auditable.

Closing-line value: the number that forward-tests an edge

Staking at the close would make CLV identically zero by construction, so bets are taken at the open. Measured in points of line, the side board averaged -1.77 points of CLV on n=15, beating the close on 20.0% of bets. Negative CLV alongside a losing record is a coherent picture: the model took the worse number and lost with it.

The total board averaged 0.57 points, beating the close on 57.1% of bets, which is consistent with a model adding nothing.

The promotion gate is written in basis points of implied probability. This capture is in points of line, and the two are not convertible without inventing an exchange rate, so that cell is left blank and both strategies stay on paper. A gate that cannot be satisfied is reported as unsatisfiable rather than quietly reinterpreted.

Three decisions worth making now

  1. Backfill weeks 1 to 8 prices. 15 games cannot distinguish an edge from noise at any rate. The capture method is proven; the constraint is call count, not uncertainty.
  2. Re-express the CLV gate in points of line. It is currently unsatisfiable by any capture, which makes it decorative. The new threshold should be fixed from the distribution of line moves, not from our own record.
  3. Keep publishing model error beside every hit rate. It is the only reason this page reports a 2-13 record as a measurement rather than a disaster, and the reason a 13-2 record was not published as skill.

The one-sentence version

The desk captured 180 real open and close prices, originated its own line on all 15 Week 9 games in the required order, graded everything against 136 official finals, and found that its own model loses: it is less accurate than the market, it underperformed its own probabilities, and the sample needed to settle the question is 783 settled bets rather than 15.

What is still missing

8 gaps are recorded in the dossier, each with how to close it. The three that block the most:

  • Direct operator prices. Everything here is a relay through one publisher. It can lag, re-round, or omit markets the operator offered.
  • Over and under prices for player props. The prop feed publishes targets and no prices, so prop combos cannot be priced at all. This is a blocked capability, not a strategy awaiting data.
  • Depth charts for 28 clubs. Requirement 6 is satisfied for four. A blank is not a zero, and the page says so.
