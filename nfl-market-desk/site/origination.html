The model is less accurate than the market on both markets. That does not make a hit rate impossible, but it means any positive result on this capture is not evidence of skill. It is evidence that 15 games is noise.

How a line is originated here

Each club is rated on points for and points against, accumulated from official final scores strictly before the graded week, and shrunk toward the league mean by 4.0 games so a two-game start cannot dominate. The projected margin is the difference of two combined attack/defence terms, plus a stated home-field allowance of 2.0 points. The total is the sum of the same terms.

The distributional assumptions are stated, not fitted: margin standard deviation 13.5 points and total standard deviation 12.5 points. Fourteen or fifteen games per week cannot identify those parameters, and tuning them on the graded weeks would be lookahead wearing a lab coat.

Anti-lookahead. The first version of this desk rated Week 9 from a standings page published after Week 9. That is lookahead and it flatters any model, because the standings already include the games being predicted. Ratings are now rebuilt per week from prior results only, and the window is printed on every row.

Basis: EC6-NFL-SCHEDULE EC6-ESPN-ODDS

Order of origination

The order is enforced by the code, not by convention, because each board is only allowed to assume what the previous one established.

  1. Side first. The only board whose fair price follows from the margin distribution alone. No assumption about scoring level is needed.
  2. Total second. Derived from the same score pair, but fitted and reported separately. It is never rescaled to make the side look better.
  3. Moneyline last. A monotone restatement of the side in price space. It is flagged as such on its own board, because counting one forecast as three independent signals is how a bad model acquires false confidence.
