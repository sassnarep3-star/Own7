Skill vs luck

An executed overdispersion test, and the exact number of settled bets required before any strategy may be called skill based.

19.03chi-square statistic, 32 df
0.966upper-tail p-value under the null
0.59variance inflation (1.0 = a coin flip)
153settled bets needed at a 60% rate, 80% power

The test, and what it says

Each club has a Pythagorean expectation from its own scoring. If that expectation were the true per-game probability and games were independent, the standardised residuals would square and sum to a chi-square with 32 degrees of freedom. The observed sum is 19.03, against an expected 32.

The statistic sits inside the null distribution, so this snapshot contains no detectable club-level variance beyond what scoring already explains. A leaderboard ranked on these 8-9 game records would be ranking noise.

The variance inflation of 0.59 deserves a sentence: it is below 1, meaning the clubs are less spread out than independent coin flips would make them. That is the opposite of the usual story where a few clubs are secretly much better. On this data, scoring already explains essentially all of the win variation.

The sample-size answer

This is the number that decides whether your leaderboard can exist yet. It is not a judgement call; it is the binomial.

A 52.4% true winner - a genuinely strong bettor - needs 2,682 settled bets before an 80%-power test can tell him apart from break-even. At 55% it is 617. At 60% it is 153. The repository contains zero settled bets.

So the answer to "remove luck from the equation" is: it cannot be removed at n=0, and it cannot be removed at n=20 either. It is removed by accumulating a dated forward record until the sample is big enough, and by nothing else.
